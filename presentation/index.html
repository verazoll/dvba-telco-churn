    Appendix 1.1

    How the five groups were found

    —

    How: a decision tree tested all 19 customer attributes and picked the yes/no questions that best separate leavers from stayers (at most 3 questions, at least 100 customers per group). Numbers = rank by revenue at risk.

    Why 15 vs 5 months? The tree picks the best cut-off in each branch. Fiber: 75% left in months 0 to 5, 60% in months 11 to 15, only 46% in months 16 to 24. DSL / basic: 42% in months 0 to 5, then 22% in months 6 to 10.

    Not in the tree ≠ irrelevant: tech support, online security and payment method shape the offers (Appendix 3.2). Long-Term Contract Customers are shown as one group (no part above 22% left). Correlations, not proven causes.

    Appendix 5.2

    Roadmap after the pilot

    Our data shows who leaves, not why. These steps close that gap and keep the contact list current.

    Prove it

    Pilot with a control group

    Half of the 939 fiber customers get the personal offer first; the other half is the comparison (Appendix 4.3).

    Learn why

    Exit survey for every leaver

    Ask each customer who cancels for the real reason (Appendix 5.1). New reasons feed back into the model and the offers.

    Stay current

    Monthly re-ranking

    Re-score all customers every month and show the contact list in the CRM, with an alert 60 days before a contract ends. Track the churn rate in the monthly review.

    Scale

    Scale what pays off

    Keep the personal offer if we keep at least 1 in 17; extend it to the other 661 top customers only if we keep at least 1 in 9 (Appendix 4.2).

    1. Logistic regression on all 19 attributes (one-hot encoded, standardized, L2 penalty), 5-fold stratified cross-validation: every customer is scored by a model that never saw them. The tree gives five explainable groups; the regression gives every customer an individual probability, which we need to rank customers and to multiply by the bill. Random forest and gradient boosting reach the same AUC (0.85), so the simpler, explainable model loses nothing. Limitation: one snapshot, so the score is read as the 12-month risk (Appendix 1.4).

    Appendix 4.3

    How the pilot proves it works

    939 fiber customers in our top 1,600
    split at random
    Offer group · ≈ 470Base email + personal Premium offer
    Control group · ≈ 470Base email only
    ↓
    Compare the churn rate of both groups: first read after 3 months
    ↓
    Kept ≥ 1 in 17control group gets the offer too; keep it in every monthly re-ranking
    Kept ≥ 1 in 9extend the personal offer to the other 661 top customers
    Kept < 1 in 17stop the personal offer, keep the Base email

    What we measure

    Share kept = (churn in control − churn in offer group) ÷ churn in control

    Why a control group

    A contact can also remind customers to cancel. Only the comparison shows the net effect of the offer.

    How precise

    With ≈ 470 per group, a gap of 9 points (49% vs 40%, keeping about 1 in 5) is detectable with 80% power.

    1. Power: two-sided test at 5% significance, churn level the model expects for these customers (49%). Smaller effects near break-even show up as a trend; we decide on the estimate and report its range. The budget stays at $28.2K, because the control group only gets the offer once it has proved itself.
