    Appendix

    Appendix 1

    Three patterns the company can act on go hand in hand with leaving

    A decision tree tested all 19 customer attributes and asked the three yes/no questions that best separate leavers from stayers. Each box shows the share who left. The five end groups are our segments (numbers = rank by revenue at risk). These are correlations, not proven causes.

    —

    The tree was limited to 3 questions and groups of at least 100 customers. All Long-Term Contract Customers are shown as one low-risk group.

    Note: Other attributes were not selected by the depth-3 decision tree. This does not mean they are irrelevant: tech support, online security and payment method inform the offers.

    Why 15 vs 5 months? The tree picks the cut-off that best separates leavers from stayers – separately for each branch, so the two thresholds differ. The data backs this up: among month-to-month fiber customers, churn stays at 60% or more up to month 15 (75% in months 0–5, 60% in months 11–15) and only then drops (46% in months 16–24). Among DSL / basic customers, it halves right after month 5 (42% in months 0–5 vs 22% in months 6–10). The thresholds are data-driven, not a business rule.

    DSL / basic = DSL or no internet service (everything that is not fiber; "basic" is our label). New: 743 customers (433 still with us) · established: 1,004 (821 still with us).

    Appendix 3 · Roadmap

    From a one-off analysis to a learning retention system

    Our data shows who leaves, not why. These steps close that gap and keep the call list current.

    Learn why

    Churn exit survey

    Ask every leaving customer for the real reason (price, service, competitor offer). Feeds new drivers back into the model.

    Listen early

    At-risk check-in

    A short satisfaction check for high-score customers, built into the retention call. Tested against a control group, since contacting some customers can also prompt them to leave.

    Stay current

    Score refreshed automatically

    The model re-scores all customers every month and on key events, and shows the call list in the sales team's CRM. New trigger: contract renewal date, with an alert 60 days before it ends.Institutionalize customer retention metrics: a churn-rate panel and a monthly review.

    Prove it

    Pilot with control group

    Run the offers on part of the call list only, compare churn with the rest, then scale what works and fix the 20% save-rate assumption.If the pilot works: extend the personal offer to the other 661 top customers – scaling threshold TBD.

    Next steps

    1

    Approve a retention pilot for the top — customers

    They hold — of the revenue at risk. Every customer gets the base email at almost no cost; phase 1 adds a personal offer (e.g. tech support + security bundle) for the 939 fiber customers among them – tested against a control group.

    Who: retention team · When: 8-week pilot · Success: lower churn than the control group

    2

    Listen: find out why (fiber) customers leave

    Fiber customers leave far more often – even at the same bill, so price alone does not explain it. Exit survey for every leaver, a short check-in survey for at-risk customers, and a review of fiber quality and support.

    Who: customer insights + network team · When: start with the pilot · Success: top 3 reasons for leaving known

    3

    Scale only if it pays off

    Phase 2: extend the personal offer to the other 661 top customers only if the pilot keeps more than 12% of would-be leavers (their break-even, 1 in 8). Then re-score all customers monthly in the CRM, with an alert 60 days before a contract ends.

    Who: Board · When: after the 8-week pilot · Success: share kept above break-even
